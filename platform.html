A living map of the business

KnowledgeMap AI is not just a chatbot, not just a data warehouse, and not just documentation. It is a living map of systems, workflows, data, files, code, rules, and people — maintained as the business changes and used to ground practical AI experiences.

KnowledgeMap Foundation

The Foundation product includes everything needed to start building and using your KnowledgeMap: enterprise context harvesting, catalogs, mappings, context packets, retrieval indexes, and grounded answers with provenance.

Built for the people who keep the business running

Every company has people who carry critical knowledge in their heads — the database person, the report builder, the integration owner, the application admin, the operations lead, the finance analyst, the purchasing coordinator, the quality manager, the customer service rep, and the person everyone asks when something breaks.

KnowledgeMap AI helps turn that scattered knowledge into a living map teams can monitor, improve, and act on — so small issues are easier to catch before they become expensive problems.

  • Database and data teams can catch data issues earlier, monitor downstream impact, and automate cleanup across tables, reports, and source systems.
  • Developers can see how code, integrations, workflows, and data flows connect before changes create downstream problems.
  • Application owners can monitor permissions, users, workflows, dependencies, and change impacts across the systems they are responsible for.
  • Reporting and analytics teams can align metric definitions, flag conflicting numbers, and trace dashboards back to trusted source systems.
  • Integration and EDI teams can monitor mappings, translation rules, interfaces, and data movement so failures are easier to prevent and fix.
  • Operations teams can track jobs, handoffs, schedules, exceptions, and process gaps before they turn into daily fire drills.
  • Customer service teams can find the right process answers, account context, escalation paths, and policy guidance without chasing tribal knowledge.
  • Purchasing and procurement teams can monitor supplier data, approvals, item records, contracts, and buying workflows for gaps or inconsistencies.
  • Finance teams can trace reports, reconciliations, cost drivers, approvals, and financial data sources before small errors become larger issues.
  • Quality teams can connect procedures, exceptions, audits, corrective actions, and compliance evidence so recurring problems are easier to spot and prevent.
  • File and knowledge owners can identify stale documents, permission risks, missing ownership, duplicate content, and knowledge gaps across shared repositories.
  • Leaders can see risks, ownership gaps, modernization opportunities, and AI-readiness signals before they become urgent initiatives.
